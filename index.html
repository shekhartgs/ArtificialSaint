The manifesto

The fire to live

Why the greatest risk of AI may not be what it does to our jobs, but what it does to our will.

Every age has its great machine. The plough freed us from hunger, the engine from distance, the computer from drudgery. Now comes a machine that can think, write, draw and decide. It is the most powerful tool we have ever built, and for the first time, the tool is capable of doing the very things that gave our lives meaning.

Every great invention lifted both maker and user. Earlier machines took over our muscle, and we moved to work of the mind. AI now reaches for the mind itself, faster than people can adapt, while its gains flow to a few who own it. It may be the first invention that lifts the few while leaving many behind, unless we choose otherwise.

So the question is not only “What can AI do?” It is “What should we still do ourselves?”

A human life is not a problem to be solved efficiently. It is a journey to be walked. We grow by struggling, learning, failing and trying again. The farmer who tends the field, the mother who teaches her child, the craftsman who masters his art: each finds purpose not in the result alone, but in the effort that earns it. Take away the effort, and the result feels empty.

This is why I worry about a world where every need is met without striving. Universal basic income and universal health income are often presented as the answer to AI taking our work. They come from a kind heart. But a safety net is not a purpose. When survival is guaranteed and nothing is asked in return, the quiet fire that pushes a person out of bed, to build, to serve, to create something great, can slowly go out. The very incentive to grow disappears. A society can be well fed and still be lost.

I am not against AI. I use it every day. I am for AI that serves human purpose, not AI that replaces it. Let it remove the boring, the dangerous and the repetitive. Let it make medicine cheaper and learning available to every child. But let us keep for ourselves the things that make us human: thinking hard, working with our hands, caring for each other, and chasing a goal bigger than ourselves.

The saints of old taught that the purpose of life is to grow, to serve and to find meaning. Machines cannot do that for us. They can only give us more time to do it, or more excuses not to.

Use the machine. Keep the fire.

Use AI wisely

Where AI helps, and where it harms

Use AI to…

  • Remove boring, repetitive work so you have time for what matters
  • Learn faster, as a patient tutor that never tires
  • Get a second opinion, then think for yourself
  • Make health care, education and services reach more people
  • Do dangerous jobs that put human lives at risk

Don’t use AI to…

  • Replace your own thinking, judgment or values
  • Stand in for real friends, family and human connection
  • Skip the struggle that builds skill and character
  • Decide what your life should mean
  • Escape effort until purpose fades away
